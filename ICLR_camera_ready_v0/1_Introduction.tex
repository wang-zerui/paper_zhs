\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Reinforcement learning (RL) has proven to be a powerful paradigm for aligning vision--language models (VLMs) with human preferences and enhancing their instruction-following capabilities~\citep{mimovl,KimiVL,GLM-4_5V,InternVL3_5,ChatGPT,Acme,rlinthewild}. A typical RL workflow for VLMs involves several distinct stages: (1) loading diverse multimodal data and prompts, (2) generating responses via policy model inference (i.e., rollouts), and (3) updating the model parameters, etc. While conceptually straightforward, scaling this process for VLMs exposes severe system-level bottlenecks across the entire pipeline, hindering efficiency and scalability.

RL training of VLMs suffers from two types of load imbalance: \textbf{(1) Vision data imbalance:} the data loading phase becomes a significant bottleneck. VLMs are trained on heterogeneous datasets containing text, high-resolution images, and video clips. The sheer volume and complexity of fetching and preprocessing such massive amounts of multimodal data require substantial I/O bandwidth and CPU compute resources. \textbf{(2) Model execution imbalance:} the model execution phase suffers from a critical load imbalance problem. A single batch of sequences can contain a mix of short image-text queries, long text-only reasoning tasks, and video inputs with tens of thousands of tokens. This extreme variation in sequence length and modality leads to a highly skewed distribution of computational and memory loads across GPUs. Consequently, some devices are overwhelmed while others remain underutilized, creating a bottleneck that stalls the entire distributed system.

Existing systems~\citep{verl, openrlhf,streamRL,slime,areal,miroRL} fail to provide a holistic solution for the VLM RL pipeline. For the vision data imbalance, current architectures often treat multimodal data reading and transformation as a centralized process. This design forces a single master node to handle heavy I/O and CPU-intensive image or video processing, failing to scale and easily causing host memory out-of-memory (OOM) errors. For the model execution imbalance, most RL frameworks are optimized for text-only models and lack sophisticated mechanisms to handle the load imbalance inherent in multimodal data. These frameworks typically employ naive sequence bucketing and packing strategies, which fail to tackle highly imbalanced workloads.
On the other hand, general-purpose large model training systems~\citep{FlexSP,WLB-LLM,Hydraulis,HotSPa,ByteScale} propose heterogeneous parallelism methods over DP instances and gradient steps, which require large batch sizes and gradient accumulation to be effective. These methods are less well covered in some settings where the effective batch size is modest or when only a few gradient steps are performed.

We present \SysName, a system designed to provide comprehensive, end-to-end optimization for the VLM RL pipeline. \SysName~deconstructs the performance bottlenecks in each stage of the RL process and introduces targeted solutions. For the data-loading bottleneck, we design ShadowLoader, a data pipeline that parallelizes expensive data fetching and preprocessing tasks across all nodes and handles only lightweight sample metadata on the single controller. Furthermore, through asynchronous materialization and prefetching, ShadowLoader eliminates data processing latency from the critical training path. This decentralizes the workload, eliminating the master-node bottleneck and significantly accelerating data throughput for large media files. For model execution imbalance, we propose FlexUlysses, a sequence-sharding mechanism. Instead of treating sequences as indivisible units, we partition them into fine-grained chunks. This allows \SysName~to balance the load at a sub-sequence level, effectively mitigating the imbalance caused by extreme length variations during both the inference and training steps. This is complemented by a specialized balancing strategy for the vision tower. \SysName~incorporates an efficient decision algorithm to determine the optimal sharding strategy for each sequence and a dynamic execution engine to orchestrate complex computation and communication patterns, maximizing hardware utilization.

We implement \SysName~on top of the veRL framework and conduct extensive experiments on two 128-GPU clusters (H800 and H200). Our updated evaluation demonstrates that by optimizing the entire RL workflow, \SysName~achieves up to an 8.47$\times$ improvement in end-to-end throughput over the baseline. 

In summary, our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We provide an end-to-end diagnosis of VLM RL training and identify two primary bottlenecks: centralized multimodal data loading and severe cross-GPU workload imbalance.
    \item We propose \textbf{ShadowLoader}, a distributed, metadata-driven data pipeline that parallelizes vision-data fetching and preprocessing across worker nodes and enables metadata-only scheduling on the controller. With prefetching and asynchronous materialization, ShadowLoader nearly breaks the data bottleneck by removing data processing and loading latency from the training critical path.
    \item We propose \textbf{FlexUlysses}, an adaptive sub-sequence sharding mechanism with a cost-aware decision model and a deadlock-free dynamic execution engine that overlaps communication and computation, achieving fine-grained load balancing for both rollout inference and model updates under extreme multimodal length skew.
    \item Our extensive evaluation on large-scale GPU clusters demonstrates that \SysName~significantly outperforms existing baselines, achieving up to an 8.47$\times$ improvement in end-to-end throughput.
\end{itemize}
