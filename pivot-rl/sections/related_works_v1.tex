\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Agentic LLM and its Training Recipes}
Agentic language models, bootstrapped with self-supervised trajectories and prompting scaffolds~\citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer}, interleave natural language with grounded actions to enable complex interaction with the environments such as tool calls~\citep{xu2023toolbench,qin2023toolllm}, embodied/text-world~\citep{shridhar2020alfworld,wang2022scienceworld}, code execution~\citep{jimenez2024swe,wang2025openhandsopenplatformai,pan2025trainingsoftwareengineeringagents}, web navigation~\citep{yao2022webshop, wei2025browsecompsimplechallengingbenchmark}, multi-domain suites~\citep{ma2024agentboard}, and social-agent environments~\citep{wang2024sotopiapi}.

Reinforcement learning optimizes an interaction policy against task-derived or preference-derived feedback~\citep{ouyang2022training,schulman2017ppo,schulman2015trpo}.
For long-horizon agents, recent work targets exploration and credit assignment via stepwise progress attribution, exploration-based trajectory optimization, and verifier/meta-reasoning rewards \citep{wang2025sparl,song2024trialerror,zhang2025rlvmr}, as well as tree-structured sampling/search and robustness to imperfect process verifiers \citep{ji2025treesearchllmrl,rohatgi2025tamingprocessverifiers}.
End-to-end multi-turn RL has been applied directly to tool-integrated reasoning and multi-tool use \citep{xue2025simpletir,dong2025toolstar}, with systems advances for scalable and stable policy optimization \citep{yang2025dcpo,yu2025dapoopensourcellmreinforcement}.
Large-scale agent training and environment design further improve the efficiency and stability of multi-turn RL for tool use \citep{luo2025agentlightningtrainai,dong2025agenticreinforcedpolicyoptimization,su2025toolorchestra}.
Hierarchical multi-turn RL frameworks such as ArCHer~\citep{zhou2024archer} explicitly separate turn-level and token-level learning signals to improve multi-turn credit assignment.
Simulated multi-turn data generation has become a common ingredient for scaling agentic supervision \citep{prabhakar2025apigenmtagenticpipelinemultiturn}.
Multi-agent extensions study coordination and credit assignment in interactive settings \citep{zhou2025sweetrl,xi2025agentgymrl}.
In parallel, RL with \emph{verifiable} rewards (RLVR) has driven large gains in domains with automatic checkers, such as math and theorem proving \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,xin2024deepseekproverv15harnessingproofassistant,Guo_2025}.

Complementary to explicit reward shaping, distillation-based post-training uses on-policy rollouts to produce more robust or efficient policies \citep{lu2025onpolicydistillation}. Recent advancements in on-policy distillation enable student models to sample their own trajectories while receiving dense, token-level supervision from a teacher, significantly mitigating the distribution mismatch seen in off-policy methods and actively improving reasoning capabilities \citep{zhao2026selfdistilledreasoneronpolicyselfdistillation,xu2025learninginteractionagenticdistillation}.
Recent work on reinforcement learning via self-distillation converts rich textual feedback (e.g., error messages) into dense learning signals without external teachers \citep{hubotter2026selfdistillation}.
Model development reports for agentic/coding systems (e.g., GLM-4.5) emphasize strict action formats and multi-stage post-training to stabilize long-horizon behavior \citep{5team2025glm45agenticreasoningcoding}, and large-scale RLVR systems such as DeepSeek-R1 highlight the effectiveness of enforcing verifiable outcomes for complex reasoning \citep{Guo_2025}.
Our work sits at this intersection: we target agentic interaction (terminal, code, browsing) while leveraging \emph{verifiability} by converting supervised trajectories into \emph{turn-level} rewards via completion-based verifiers, enabling on-policy optimization without a separate reward model.


\subsection{Adapting Behavior Cloning into RL}

A persistent issue in LLM post-training, particularly during purely offline supervised updates, is catastrophic forgetting: optimizing on a narrow target distribution can degrade general capabilities \citep{luo2023empiricalCF,li2024revisitingCF,imanov2026mechanisticCF}. These findings connect to the broader continual-learning literature \citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc,li2016lwf} and the \emph{superficial alignment hypothesis} \citep{zhou2023limaalignment,vergarabrowne2026sahcomplexity,raghavendra2024revisitingSAH}.  Theoretical work establishes that offline imitation learning without environment interaction inherently suffers from suboptimality that grows quadratically with the task horizon \citep{rajaraman2020towardfundamentallimitsimitation}. Overcoming these fundamental limits necessitates interacting with the environment, motivating on-policy data aggregation methods such as DAgger \citep{ross2011dagger}, and variance-reduction techniques like replay estimation that smooth expert moment matching via online simulation \citep{swamy2022minimaxoptimalonlineimitation}.

Importantly, online/on-policy RL has been shown to mitigate forgetting compared to purely offline updates, retaining broader capabilities through continued environment interaction \citep{shenfeld2025rlsrazoronlinereinforcement,chen2025retainingdoingroleonpolicy}. 
To effectively transfer knowledge from offline demonstrations into robust online performance, RL-from-demonstrations approaches combine expert data with RL updates, using a pre-existing guide policy to form a curriculum of starting states for online exploration \citep{uchendu2022jumpstartreinforcementlearning}, or integrating offline data with RL updates as in DQfD \citep{hester2018dqfd}. Recent work also explores using partial successful traces as \emph{prefixes} to guide on-policy optimization \citep{setlur2026prefixrl}.
By performing Monte Carlo rollouts from the current policy and using the ground-truth SFT token as a reward signal, \citet{ming2025onetokenrollout} repurpose off-policy supervised datasets into on-policy RL signals.
Our approach is closest in spirit to this SFT-to-RL bridge, but using turn-based pivot selection and functional rewards to specialize in multi-turn tool use.
